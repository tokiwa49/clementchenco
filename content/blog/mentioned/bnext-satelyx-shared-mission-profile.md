Meet/bnext published a dedicated profile of SATELYX on October 21, written by 曾令懷. The piece looks at the cost of proving a product in orbit and the case for sharing a satellite mission across several technology teams.

<!--more-->

The article quotes me on the semiconductor foundry comparison: separating chip design from manufacturing gave designers room to build without having to fund a factory of their own. I see a similar need in space, where developing a component can also mean taking on the cost and work of an entire satellite just to test it.

The profile also explores the ambition behind repeated missions: building a catalogue of technologies with flight evidence that can later be combined into complete solutions. It discusses Japan's demand for in-orbit testing and Taiwan's role as a hardware supply base.

This follows [Bnext's earlier feature on Taiwanese space startups](/blog/mentioned/bnext-taiwan-space-startups-satelyx/), with more detail on SATELYX's business model and how I moved from venture capital into the team.
