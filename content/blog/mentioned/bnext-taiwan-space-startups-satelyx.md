Meet/bnext published a feature on October 14 on Taiwanese space startups that are skipping rocket-building entirely, built around SATELYX's in-orbit verification business and Izumi Space's lunar-soil construction material, set against SpaceX's launch-cost cuts as the backdrop that made both viable.

<!--more-->

The piece, written by 曾令懷, quotes me on the supply-demand gap that SATELYX is built to close: Japan's space agency offers roughly 8 to 15 in-orbit verification slots a year, against around 190 programs that need testing. A full verification mission can run into the millions of dollars for a startup or lab working alone, which is why most of that demand goes unmet. I compared SATELYX's position to a semiconductor foundry: the goal isn't to build the most capable satellite, it's to run a standardized verification line that other hardware and software teams can use without each having to build and fly their own test mission.

The article also covers Izumi Space's work turning lunar regolith into construction material, and names several Taiwanese electronics suppliers (昇達科, 華通, 穩懋, 群創, 啟碁, 同欣電, 台揚) as part of the broader supply chain the piece ties to Taiwan's space ambitions.

Full article (Chinese): [Meet/bnext, October 14, 2026](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/92627/spacex-space-startups-satelyx-izumi-space).
